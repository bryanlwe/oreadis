Mount Cook National Park, New Zealand

Overview

Mount Cook National Park is one of the easiest alpine areas in New Zealand to access without needing serious mountaineering experience. I based myself in Mount Cook Village for 3 nights and spent most of the trip hiking, walking around the glacier lakes, and watching the weather change around the Southern Alps.

The scenery feels much bigger and rougher than Queenstown or Wanaka. Huge glacier valleys, fast-changing weather, and steep rock walls everywhere. Even short walks feel dramatic here. Most people come for the Hooker Valley Track, but there are enough day hikes and viewpoints to comfortably fill 2–4 days.

I spent around 220 NZD per day including hostel accommodation, food, fuel, and activities. It is easy to do cheaper with a campervan or by cooking all your meals.

Need to Know
  • Weather changes fast even in summer. A clear morning can turn into rain, wind, or low cloud by afternoon. Check forecasts daily and build flexibility into your plans.
  • Accommodation inside Mount Cook Village books out early during summer and long weekends. Book several weeks ahead if travelling December through February.
  • There is almost no supermarket shopping once you leave Twizel. Buy groceries and fuel before entering the park — there is no petrol station inside Mount Cook Village.
  • Mobile reception is limited inside the national park. Some tracks have no signal at all.
  • Sandflies are common near water and around sunset. Bring repellent.
  • Hooker Valley Track gets crowded from mid-morning onwards. Start before 8 AM for quieter conditions and easier parking.
What to Do
  • Hooker Valley Track is the classic walk in the park. The return hike is around 10 km with roughly 150 m elevation gain and takes 2.5–3.5 hours. The track crosses three suspension bridges and finishes at Hooker Lake with views of Aoraki / Mount Cook. Go early or late in the day to avoid crowds.
  • Sealy Tarns Track is the hardest short hike accessible from the village. The return walk is roughly 5.8 km with around 600 m elevation gain almost entirely on stairs. Expect 2–4 hours depending on fitness. The viewpoint looks directly over Mueller Glacier and the Hooker Valley.
  • Mueller Hut Route is a serious full-day hike or overnight option for fit hikers. The return trip is around 14 km with roughly 1,050 m elevation gain and takes 6–8 hours. The final section is rocky and exposed in bad weather. Snow can remain well into summer.
  • Tasman Glacier View Track is a short 20-minute walk to viewpoints over Tasman Lake and the glacier terminal face. You can often see small icebergs floating in the lake.
  • Blue Lakes and Tasman Lake are easy walks near the Tasman Glacier car park. The lakes are now mostly filled with sediment but the area gives good glacier valley views and access to the lakeshore.
  • Kea Point Track is a quieter alternative to Hooker Valley. The return walk is around 3 km and takes about 1 hour, with views over Mueller Glacier Lake.
  • Stargazing around Mount Cook Village is excellent on clear nights. The area sits inside the Aoraki Mackenzie International Dark Sky Reserve, one of the darkest sky reserves in the world.

Getting There
  • Driving from Queenstown takes around 3.5–4 hours via Lindis Pass and Twizel. Fuel up in Twizel before the final stretch — there is no petrol station inside the village.
  • Driving from Christchurch takes around 4.5 hours.
  • The drive along Mount Cook Road beside Lake Pukaki is one of the best road sections in New Zealand. Allow extra time for stops.
  • Intercity and GreatSights buses run services to Mount Cook Village from Christchurch and Queenstown, though services can be limited outside peak season.
Getting Around
  • A car is the easiest option. Most trailheads are within a 5–15 minute drive from Mount Cook Village.
  • The village itself is walkable — the main visitor centre, accommodation, and several tracks connect directly from town.
  • Parking at Hooker Valley and Tasman Glacier fills quickly from late morning in peak season. Arrive early or expect a walk from overflow areas.
  • Hitchhiking between trailheads is fairly common during summer due to limited parking and a cooperative atmosphere among hikers.
Where to Stay
  • Mount Cook Village has hostels, lodges, and hotel-style accommodation. Expect roughly 50–80 NZD for hostel dorms and 250+ NZD for private hotel rooms in summer.
  • White Horse Hill Campground is the main camping option, near the Hooker Valley Track trailhead. Facilities are basic but the location is excellent for early starts.
  • Twizel is a cheaper base around 50 minutes away with more supermarkets, fuel, and budget accommodation. Useful if the village is fully booked.

Food or market photo caption

Eat and Drink
  • Most people self-cater here because dining options are limited and expensive. Stock up on groceries in Twizel before arriving — there are only small convenience-style stores inside the park.
  • Café meals inside Mount Cook Village run around 20–35 NZD and restaurant mains around 35–50 NZD. The Hermitage complex has the largest concentration of restaurants and cafés in the area.
  • Coffee costs around 6 NZD at most spots in the village.

Day-trip photo caption

Day Hikes / Trips and Walks
  • Red Tarns Track is a shorter alternative to Sealy Tarns. The return walk is around 2 km with roughly 300 m elevation gain and takes 1–2 hours.
  • Lake Pukaki shoreline stops along Mount Cook Road are worth doing on the drive in or out. The bright blue glacial water looks best in full sun — allow extra time for a proper stop rather than a drive-by.
  • Tasman Glacier Lake boat tours run seasonally and get close to floating glacier icebergs. Expensive but a different experience from the walking viewpoints if conditions are good.
